# Playtest 1: critic pass on `feat/realism-pass`

Date: 2026-09-27 · Build: `3ac03d4` (lighting, glass HUD, breath/O2/Condition, breach, blows, streamed Monterey seafloor)
Method: 24 headless runs of `tools/playtest.mjs` (SwiftShader, about 3 fps), plus scripted `steps` runs and offline checks of the bathymetry assets. Screenshots are in `.playtest/critic/<run>/`. Visuals, poses and logic were judged. Frame rate was not.

---

## Verdict

This is not yet Blue Planet, and it is not yet a game loop. It is a handsome empty blue room with an airplane-shaped whale in it. The lighting near the surface is the strongest asset: god rays, caustics on the back, the depth gradient and the live-ocean menu are close to the brief. Almost everything the player is supposed to *hunt* and *navigate by* can't be seen, though. The krill swarm is invisible beyond about 15 m and at its 55 m daytime depth. The Monterey seafloor is invisible from normal play. Night never gets dark. The one piece of "real Monterey" that ships, the bathymetry, is geometrically wrong: every level is squashed north–south by about 0.8, and there are cliffs of 50–550 m at every L0 tile row seam, one of them about 660 m south of the spawn point. On the model side, the sperm whale is a rorqual clone. All three species have plank flippers, a rolling-pin fluke and a hoop for a dorsal fin. On the loop side, the rorqual filter phase is dead code, a patch regrows faster than a whale can digest it, the surface interval lasts about 8 s, the "deep breath" is slower than a normal breath, and nothing happens when Condition hits 0 or the target. The architecture is right and the numbers are close. The next pass should make the prey visible, make the world true and give the day an ending.

| Stomach / Condition | 420 krill = +8.4 Condition; digests in 90 s; metabolism 0.72/min | Max net about +4.9/min, so 40 → 80 takes ≥ 8 min of perfect eating. That's a sensible session length, but there is no ending (#9) |
| Surge | 150 krill; bonus at ≥ 40 per lunge | A 2.4 m mouth sweeping about 8 m through a cloud of 0.13 krill/m³ gets about 15–20 per lunge, so the ×1.5 bonus is nearly unreachable. About 3 dives per breach is fine |
| Regrow | 7.5 krill/s per patch | Faster than digestion, so patches never deplete (#9) |
| Blackout | Forced climb at 0.7 cruise; recovers at O2 ≥ 35 % (2 blows); −25 Condition | Works, with no soft-lock observed. Escape from 55 m takes about 25 s. A blackout at Condition 0 has no teeth |

No soft-locks or console errors were seen in 24 runs. There are no shore dead-ends at spawn (it's deep water). The shore push-back reverses the whale's velocity, which works but feels like a wall. The floor clamp checks only the centre and the head, so on steep walls the flippers and tail can clip into the slope.

## What's already great

- **The near-surface light.** God rays, caustics on the whale's back (`side/humpback-side-0.png`) and the depth-to-navy gradient look filmic.
- **The menu.** A live whale in the water, restrained type and real facts (`menu/humpback-menu.png`) fit "ink on glass" exactly.
- **The blows are species-true in silhouette.** The blue's tall narrow column against the humpback's bushy puff is right (`blow-blue/blue-blow-11.png`, `blow-humpback/humpback-blow-11.png`).
- **The breach arc physics.** A real-g ballistic arc gives 79 % clearance, a 184° twist and slow-motion at the apex. The pitch-over and roll onto the back read correctly in `breach2/humpback-air-2..8`.
- **The streaming and floating origin.** A 5 km round trip drifts less than 1 m, and the rebase plumbing is clean. Only the data is wrong (#1).
- **The HUD.** It is quiet, hairline and legible over bright water (`hud/humpback-hud-1-bright.png`), and the pause screen is tasteful.

## Next 5 biggest realism wins

1. **Make the swarm a visible, volumetric thing** (#2, #13, #15). A rust-brown haze wall at 50 m that the whale carves through is the game's money shot, and it also fixes the "can't find krill" dead end and 90 % of the triangle budget.
2. **Fix and reveal the real Monterey** (#1, #7). Refetch the bathymetry with the correct aspect. Give the floor sediment colour, ripples and rock on the walls, and put the spawn on the rim where the canyon drop is visible in the first 30 s.
3. **Rebuild the appendages and the sperm whale** (#3, #4). Tapered, scalloped white humpback flippers, a thin notched fluke, a proper dorsal hump, ventral pleats and a box-headed sperm whale. The silhouette is what viewers recognise from Blue Planet, and right now it says "plane".
4. **Monterey water and time of day** (#5, #7). Green-grey plankton water with short visibility by day, and a genuinely dark night with bioluminescent krill. This also makes the DVM mechanic *visible*.
5. **A breach that looks like footage** (#8, #11, #17). Sheet water streaming off the body, a mist plume, a crown curtain and a foam footprint, on a sea with swell and glitter. It is the loop's payoff and should be the best-looking 3 seconds in the game.
